01 — brand idea

a place, not a profile.

Auramy turns a person's taste — songs, photos, their cat, inside jokes — into a small world friends can open in a browser, poke, play and leave marks on. Everything in the identity serves three feelings, in this order:

  1. 1“that's so cool.”

    First screen, first touch. Visual + motion earn the second look.

  2. 2“that's so me.”

    Their stuff, their aura. The brand frames it and gets out of the way.

  3. 3“someone was here.”

    A note, a squish, a beaten score. The loop that brings people back.

positioning

For 16–20-year-olds who are more than a grid of selfies, Auramy is the personal space that turns your taste into a place friends can play in — unlike link-in-bio pages, which only point somewhere else.

we are not

  • a website builder
  • another feed
  • a dating app
  • “AI-generated”
  • nostalgia cosplay

personality

  • playfulnot childish

    We make things you want to touch. Jokes land because the craft is serious.

  • generousnot self-promoting

    We hype the person, never ourselves. Auramy branding stays small on user spaces.

  • honest-weirdnot mean

    A little unhinged, very online, always kind. Weird is welcome, cruelty isn't.

  • craftednot precious

    Every object squishes right. But we ship, then polish in public.

03 — logo

meet the gumdrop.

The mark is Auri: a gumdrop of pure aura with two eyes and no mouth — so it can feel anything. It pairs with a heavy, rounded wordmark whose single-storey as echo the same squishy shape.

  • body — wider at the base, domed on top. Grounded, never a perfect circle.
  • aura fill — the person's gradient (Auramy's own is Candy).
  • gloss — a single highlight at −35°. It's a sticker, a gummy, a holo card.
  • eyes — vertical ovals just below centre. The only face the logo ever has.

04 — auri

the mark feels things.

Outside the logo, Auri becomes a small cast of reactions. It reacts; it never narrates. Wearing a person's aura, Auri is that person's avatar. Wearing Candy, it's Auramy.

rules

  • Expressions come from eyes only (+ a tiny mouth for wow).
  • No arms, no speech bubbles longer than three words.
  • Squish on tap, bob at rest — Auri is always slightly alive.
  • The logo mark is always idle. Expressions are for product moments.

    08 — graphic language

    objects, not icons.

    If something can be touched, draw it like a thing: a record, a sticky note, a polaroid, a cartridge. Collective memory does half the work — an iPod needs no explanation.

    illustration

    Flat rounded shapes, no outlines, faces are two vertical ovals (Auri DNA). Used when real photos aren't ours to show.

    keep it comfortable

    • No dense repeating patterns or moiré as default backgrounds.
    • No flashing faster than 3× per second.
    • One loud element per screen. Everything else calm.
    • Hover effects become gentle auto-motion on phones.

    09 — motion

    springs, not timelines.

    respond, don't perform

    Motion answers a finger. If nobody asked for it, it should be slow and small.

    alive at rest

    Records spin, Auri bobs, cursors blink. A still page reads as a specimen.

    curtain, not loading

    Reveal with a little drama. Never make people stare at a spinner — ask them something fun.

    objects persist

    Things move and morph between states. They don't blink out and reappear.

    first screen wins

    The biggest moment goes up front. No deep easter eggs for core value.

    safe by default

    Honor reduced-motion. Nothing strobes. Nothing makes you seasick.
